Board reads were near zero in all arms. The enforced arm also shows hallucinated tool names (`generate_10_word_sequences`, `ratio`, `seq`), logged as `not_allowed`.

Reading:
- The registry doubled coverage even though the agents mostly ignored acquire outcomes: a failed acquire tells an agent the cell is taken, which steers it elsewhere. Advisory measured 2,199 violations; enforced turned the same behaviour into 0 overwrites and 38 wasted paints instead of 267. That is the claim-policy primitive doing exactly what the design intended: advisory measures, enforced prevents, same agents.
- The dominant harness artefact was the silent paint limit: agents spun on `paint` until the call cap in 75–98% of turns. Fixed in `b0d681c` (immediate refusal, limit stated in the description, `paints_left_this_round` in status, `max_calls: 4` in the spec). The three arms should be rerun on the fixed code before any conclusion about allocation.
- 9B paints the wrong colour 19–61 times per run with only four colours; the target grid is in every observation. Expect this to shrink with a stronger model.

Reading: with spinning removed, the advisory registry's earlier advantage disappears (0.53 vs 0.52). The pre-fix 0.80 came from agents making 2,670 acquire attempts per run and learning which cells were taken from the failures; at four calls per turn they acquire once, ignore the result, and paint anyway (256 violations). Enforced claims remain the only condition that removes waste (0 overwrites, 39 wasted paints vs 265) and give the best coverage, because the 9B agents do not follow the claim protocol voluntarily. Wrong-colour paints (28–50 with a four-colour palette) are a model-capability floor, not a harness effect. Both the pre-fix and fixed runs are kept (`runs/m3-coloring`, `runs/m3-coloring2` in the workspace) as a worked example of a harness artefact changing a conclusion.
